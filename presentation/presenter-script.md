# Lunar Echoes: Presenter Script

Written for the team's 13-slide deck (`Lunar Echoes1234.pdf`, made in Canva).
**Length as written: about 5–5½ minutes**: about 4 minutes of talk (~570 words at a calm 140 words per
minute) plus a 60–90 second live demo.

**If your time limit is shorter**, cut in this order:
1. Slides 6–8 (≈25 s): skip their lines, or remove the slides.
2. Slide 10 (≈15 s): say one line only: "Two very different worlds; every number here is from NASA's fact sheets."
3. Demo steps 3 and 5 (≈30 s).
4. Slide 11 (≈20 s): keep only the last two sentences.

That brings it down to about **3½ minutes**.

How to use it:
- **Bold** = say this with emphasis. *[Click]* = next slide or next animation.
- *(Speaker: …)* is a suggestion based on the team roles on slide 1. Change it to suit the team.
- Every fact here is checked in `docs/research-sources.md`. If you change a number, check it there first.
- Speak slowly. Pause after each bold line. Pauses make the story land.

## Slide 3: Our idea (≈25 s)
*(Speaker: Storyteller)*

> So we asked: what if every machine could tell its own story?
> **Every machine is an echo of a mission.**
> Lunar Echoes is a free digital museum. Every object is told in the same five chapters:
> **Designed**, why it was built. **Mission**, what it did. **Discovery**, what it helped us learn.
> **Left behind**, what happened to it. And **Legacy**, where it stands today.

*[Click]*

## Slide 4: The collection (≈20 s)
*(Speaker: Researcher)*

> Our collection has **twelve objects: six on the Moon and six on Mars**, arriving between 1967 and 2018.
> From the robot lander Surveyor 3, to Apollo 11's descent stage, to the rovers Sojourner, Spirit and Opportunity,
> to the InSight lander that listened for marsquakes.
> Eleven of them are now silent. **Only one is still used for science.**

*[Click]*

## Slide 5: A mirror that still answers (≈25 s)
*(Speaker: Storyteller)*

> This is it. In **July 1969**, Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin set up a panel of **100 small mirrors** on the Moon.
> It needs **no power**, so it never switched off.
> Observatories on Earth still fire laser pulses at it and time the light coming back.
> That's how we know the Moon is drifting away from Earth by **3.8 centimetres every year.**
> More than 57 years later, **it still answers.**

*[Click]*

## Slide 9: InSight: then vs now (≈25 s)
*(Speaker: Researcher)*

> InSight landed on Mars in **November 2018** to listen inside the planet.
> Its seismometer recorded **1,319 marsquakes**. They showed that Mars has a **molten core**, bigger than expected.
> Slowly, dust covered its solar panels. It last talked to Earth on **15 December 2022.**
> Then, in 2024, a NASA orbiter photographed it again: the lander has turned
> **the same reddish-brown colour as Mars itself.**

*[Pause.] [Click]*

---

## Slide 10: Moon vs Mars (≈15 s)
*(Speaker: Researcher)*

> These are two very different places to leave a machine.
> On the Moon, gravity is about **one-sixth** of Earth's and a day lasts **29 and a half Earth days**.
> On Mars, gravity is about **two-fifths** of Earth's, and a day is **24 hours and 40 minutes**.
> All of these numbers come from NASA's own fact sheets.

*[Click]*

## Slide 11: What the website does (≈20 s)
*(Speaker: Developer)*

> Lunar Echoes lets you explore by world, read each object's story in five chapters,
> follow its mission journey, compare *then and now*, compare the Moon and Mars, and see where each object rests.
> The quiz doesn't just test you. **After every answer, it tells you the story behind it.**
> **Every fact links to its NASA source**, mostly NASA's NSSDCA catalogue, the dataset this challenge lists.
> Where NASA gives no verified value, we show "Not available" instead of guessing.

## Likely judge questions (short answers)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Where does your data come from? | Mainly the **NASA NSSDCA Master Catalog**, which the challenge lists, plus NASA Science mission pages and the NASA Image and Video Library. Every object links to its source. |
| How do you make sure facts are correct? | Every fact is matched to a NASA page in our research sources document. If NASA gives no verified value, we write "Not available". |
| Why only 12 objects? | We chose depth over breadth: each object has a full five-chapter story. The design makes adding more easy, and Viking 2 and the Ingenuity helicopter are next. |
| Who is it for? | School-age explorers, roughly 9 to 15, and their teachers. |
| Did you use AI? | Yes, for code and for gathering facts from NASA pages. It's disclosed in our project. Every fact is linked to its NASA source, and the team checked them. *[Only say "checked" once you have.]* |
| Does it work without internet? | Yes. The NASA photos are stored with the site, so it runs offline. |
| Is the mirror really still used? | Yes. NASA describes lunar laser ranging as ongoing, with more than 57 years of continuous data. |
