Silent Monuments of Human Exploration

Since the dawn of the Space Age in the 1960s, humanity has sent robotic probes and daring astronauts to touch the surfaces of other worlds. When missions end and fuel runs dry, these extraordinary machines remain behind—becoming silent time capsules and scientific monuments across the extraterrestrial landscape.

Lunar Echoes uncovers the untold stories of NASA's historic descent stages, moon buggies, supersonic parachutes, and sky cranes resting on the Moon and Mars. Grounded strictly in official NASA datasets, our project translates technical telemetry into vivid narratives for the next generation of space explorers.

    NASA Data References & Acknowledgments

    Built for the NASA International Space Apps Challenge 2026. Every fact, mission date, coordinate, and status in Lunar Echoes is verified and referenced in docs/research-sources.md.

    • NASA Space Science Data Coordinated Archive (NSSDCA): nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov — Official Challenge Dataset for mission parameters and spacecraft master catalog.
    • NASA Planetary Data System (PDS): pds.nasa.gov — Planetary science archives and surface coordinate frames.
    • Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera (LROC): lroc.asu.edu — High-resolution lunar surface imagery and landing site coordinate references (Wagner et al., 2017).
    • Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter HiRISE: uahirise.org — Sub-meter orbital imagery of Martian descent stages, parachutes, and blast scars.
    • NASA Image and Video Library: images.nasa.gov & JPL Photojournal: photojournal.jpl.nasa.gov — Public domain NASA mission photography and aerial surveys.

    This project strictly adheres to NASA Space Apps Challenge branding and open science guidelines. No unverified facts were used.
